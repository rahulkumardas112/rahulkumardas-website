About the journal

Polymathica is a quarterly, double-blind peer-reviewed, open-access journal that publishes original research across the humanities, social sciences, commerce and management, natural sciences, and technology. It gives particular space to work that crosses disciplinary boundaries.

Many of today's questions do not belong to one field. A study of startup survival draws on finance, sociology and public policy. Climate adaptation in a river valley needs ecology, economics and local history. Polymathica exists for research of this kind, and for strong single-discipline work that speaks to readers beyond its own field.

The journal is based in Assam, in Northeast India, and welcomes submissions from researchers everywhere. We especially encourage early-career scholars, PhD candidates and researchers from institutions in the Northeast and other under-represented regions.

Aims and scope

Polymathica aims to publish rigorous, readable research that connects ideas across disciplines and contributes to theory, policy and practice.

Aims

  • To provide an open, high-quality outlet for multidisciplinary and interdisciplinary research.
  • To encourage dialogue between disciplines that rarely publish side by side.
  • To support early-career researchers through constructive, developmental peer review.
  • To bring research on Northeast India and comparable regions into wider scholarly conversation.
  • To make all published research freely and permanently accessible.

What we look for

Submissions should make a clear contribution, use sound and transparent methods, and engage with relevant literature. Interdisciplinary papers should explain how combining perspectives produces insight that a single discipline could not. Papers must be written so that an educated reader from outside the field can follow the argument.

Outside our scope

We do not publish purely descriptive reports without analysis, opinion pieces without scholarly grounding, or work already published or under review elsewhere.

Anonymising your manuscript

Because review is double-blind, remove author names, affiliations and self-identifying references from the main file. Cite your own earlier work in the third person, and clear author details from the document properties.

Peer review process

Polymathica uses double-blind peer review: reviewers do not know the authors' identities, and authors do not know the reviewers'.

  1. Submission and acknowledgementThe editorial office confirms receipt and assigns a manuscript ID within 3 working days.
  2. Initial screeningThe editor checks scope, formatting, anonymisation and similarity. Papers that fail are returned within 7 days.
  3. Reviewer assignmentThe handling editor invites at least two independent experts in the field.
  4. ReviewReviewers assess originality, methods, contribution and clarity, usually within 3–4 weeks.
  5. DecisionThe editor makes one of four decisions: accept, minor revision, major revision or reject.
  6. RevisionAuthors revise and respond to each comment point by point, within 3 weeks for minor and 6 weeks for major revisions.
  7. Acceptance and productionAccepted papers are copyedited, typeset and sent to authors for proof checking before publication.

Reviewer criteria

  • Is the research question clear and significant?
  • Are the methods appropriate and described in enough detail to be replicated?
  • Are the conclusions supported by the evidence?
  • Does the paper engage with current literature?
  • Is it clearly written for a multidisciplinary audience?

Appeals

Authors may appeal a decision once, in writing, within 30 days. Appeals are reviewed by an editor not involved in the original decision.

Guidelines for reviewers

Reviewers are the foundation of the journal's quality. Thank you for giving your time and expertise.

Before you accept

  • Accept only if the manuscript is within your expertise and you can finish the review within 3–4 weeks.
  • Decline if you have a conflict of interest, or if you think you can identify the authors.

Writing your review

  1. Summarise the paperIn two or three sentences, state what the paper claims and how. This shows the authors you understood their work.
  2. Assess the contributionIs the question important? Does the paper add something new to the literature?
  3. Check the methodsAre the data, sample and analysis appropriate and clearly described? Could another researcher repeat the study?
  4. Test the conclusionsDo the findings follow from the evidence, and are limitations acknowledged?
  5. Comment on clarityIs the paper readable for scholars outside its field? Are tables and figures clear?
  6. Make a recommendationAccept, minor revision, major revision or reject, with reasons the editor can follow.

Tone and confidentiality

  • Be specific and constructive. Point out problems and suggest how to fix them.
  • Keep the manuscript confidential and do not share it or upload it to AI tools.
  • Do not contact the authors directly.

Recognition

Reviewers receive a certificate of reviewing for each completed review and are acknowledged annually on this website.

Frequently asked questions

Does Polymathica charge any fee?

No. There is no submission fee, review fee or article processing charge. Reading is also free.

Is the journal peer reviewed?

Yes. Every research article is reviewed by at least two independent experts under double-blind review.

Does the journal have an ISSN?

The journal will apply for an e-ISSN from the ISSN National Centre India after its first issue is published. The number will be shown here once assigned.

Is the journal indexed?

Not yet. The journal will apply to Google Scholar, Crossref, DOAJ and other databases as it meets each service's requirements. We will not claim any indexing that has not been granted.

How long does review take?

We aim for a first decision within 6 weeks. The full process, including revision, usually takes 2–3 months.

Can I submit a paper presented at a conference?

Yes, if it has not been published in full in proceedings. Mention the conference in your cover letter.

Can research scholars submit?

Yes. PhD scholars are welcome, and we encourage them to submit with their supervisors where appropriate.

Will I get a publication certificate?

Yes. Each author of a published article receives a certificate of publication.

Who owns the copyright?

Publication ethics and policies

Polymathica follows the Core Practices of the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) for authors, editors and reviewers.

Open access statement

All content is freely available without charge to readers or their institutions. Readers may read, download, copy, distribute, print, search or link to the full texts of articles without asking prior permission from the publisher or the author, in line with the Budapest Open Access Initiative definition of open access.

Plagiarism

All submissions are checked with similarity-detection software. Manuscripts with a similarity index above 10% (excluding references) or any evidence of copied text, data or ideas are rejected. Plagiarism discovered after publication leads to retraction.

Authorship

Everyone listed as an author must have made a substantial contribution to the work and approved the final version. Changes to the author list after submission require written agreement from all authors.

Use of AI tools

AI tools cannot be listed as authors. Authors who use AI tools for language editing, analysis or drafting must state how they were used in the methods or acknowledgements section. Authors remain fully responsible for the accuracy and originality of the content. Reviewers must not upload manuscripts to AI tools.

Conflicts of interest

Authors, reviewers and editors must declare any financial or personal relationship that could influence the work. Editors do not handle manuscripts from their own institution's colleagues, students or co-authors.

Research ethics

Research involving human participants must have informed consent and, where required, approval from an institutional ethics committee. Participant data must be anonymised.

Data availability

Authors are encouraged to share data and code in a public repository, or to explain in a data availability statement why this is not possible.

Corrections and retractions

Errors that affect the findings are corrected with a published correction notice. Articles with serious misconduct or unreliable findings are retracted, with the original kept online and clearly marked.

Copyright and licence

Authors keep copyright. Articles are published under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0), which allows anyone to share and adapt the work with proper credit.

Archiving

Published content will be deposited with a long-term digital preservation service so it stays available if the journal ceases publication.
